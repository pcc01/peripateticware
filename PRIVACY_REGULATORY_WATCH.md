## Where this would land in the codebase (read this first)

The compliance engine already has dormant, purpose-built infrastructure for exactly this kind of finding — it does not need a new mechanism, only data:

- `backend/models/compliance.py`: `compliance_rules.ai_student_permitted` and `compliance_rules.ai_teacher_permitted` (boolean columns, `default=True`), added by `backend/alembic/versions/20260530_compliance_rules_regulation_type.py`, alongside a `regulation_type` column (`'privacy'` vs `'ai'`) that splits AI rules out from privacy rules.
- `backend/routes/privacy.py`, `GET /api/v1/privacy/status` (~lines 308–405): already computes `ai_student_permitted`/`ai_teacher_permitted` (strictest-wins across active `regulation_type='ai'` rows), `education_ai_classification` (read from `rule_definition["education_classification"]` on an AI rule), and `active_ai_regulations` (per-jurisdiction list of AI rules with their permission flags).
- **Confirmed by direct inspection today: no seed script has ever inserted a `compliance_rules` row with `regulation_type='ai'`.** `ai_rules` is always empty at runtime, so `ai_student_permitted`/`ai_teacher_permitted` always default to `True`, `education_ai_classification` is always `None`, and `active_ai_regulations` is always `[]`. The mechanism is real; the data behind it is not.
- `backend/config/jurisdictions/gdpr_eu.json` carries `"eu_ai_act_applies": true`. Confirmed by grep: `backend/services/privacy_engine.py` never reads `eu_ai_act_applies` anywhere — it is a dangling flag with no code path.

Any of the findings below that warrant enforcement would be implemented by seeding `regulation_type='ai'` compliance rules (with `ai_student_permitted`/`ai_teacher_permitted` and a `rule_definition.education_classification`), not by inventing new columns or endpoints. That implementation work is explicitly out of scope for this document.

## 1. US schools/districts/states restricting AI in K-12

### Headline finding: NYC DOE generative-AI moratorium (announced Sept 2, 2026)

New York City — the largest US school district (~600,000 K-8 students affected) — imposed a one-year moratorium banning student-facing generative AI for grades 2K through 8, and banned **"companion chatbots" district-wide across all grades with no grade exemption, no pilot, and no supervised path**. High schools retain access to five vetted tools (Quill, Edia, Brisk Teaching, Playlab, Intel AI-Ready Schools), capped at 50,000 students and five classes per school. The district is simultaneously "discontinuing or disabling the AI components of more than 38 previously allowed [vendor] programs" that don't meet the new standard, and teachers are barred from using AI to grade work, decide promotion/graduation, or handle student crisis situations.

- This is **administrative/mayoral-office and DOE policy, not state legislation** — announced jointly by the Mayor's office and the Schools Chancellor for the 2026-27 school year, not a bill.
- It does not draw an explicit line between an open-ended AI chat and a fixed/curated on-device question bank — the ban's language targets "generative AI" and "companion chatbots" as categories, not by underlying architecture. A `curated_only` mode with no live model call is very likely outside its scope; an `ai_chat` mode with a live LLM-backed conversational assistant matches the banned category exactly, for 2K-8, and the "companion chatbot" language reads as also covering it in high school absent a vetted-tool carve-out.
- Sources: [Chalkbeat NY](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/09/02/nyc-schools-to-set-ai-policy-ban-screen-time-limits/), [NYC Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/mayor-mamdani-and-chancellor-samuels-put-students-first-with-nat), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/3/nycs-ai-ban-through-grade-8-sets-stage-for-rest-of-us-experts-say), [BetaNYC policy breakdown](https://www.beta.nyc/2026/09/04/nyc-paused-ai-in-its-classrooms-here-is-what-the-policy-actually-says/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/02/tech/new-york-city-classroom-ai-ban) (blocked from direct fetch, HTTP 451, but corroborated by the above).

**Applies to this product?** Directly, if Peripateticware is or could be sold into NYC DOE (or any district that follows its lead — experts quoted in the Al Jazeera piece expect it to set a national template). This is the single most concrete, product-relevant finding in this document: it is a real, currently-effective restriction on the exact feature (`ai_chat`) this platform ships, tied to the exact age bands (`under_13`/`under_16` roughly map to "2K-8") the compliance engine already tracks.

### State legislative/guidance activity (broader, mostly not bans)

Most 2026 state activity establishes **procurement, disclosure, and oversight duties for districts and vendors** rather than banning AI chat outright:

- **Maryland** — AI Ready Schools Act: state education department must issue/maintain AI guidance, districts must adopt aligned policies and name AI coordinators, procurement must align to state guidance.
- **Illinois** — ISBE statewide AI framework (June 2026), guidance-only, not a mandate.
- **Idaho** — SB 1227/local-policy law: districts must adopt local AI-use policies; state must develop a generative-AI framework (privacy, procurement, transparency, AI literacy); a March 2026 law separately guarantees AI cannot "replace or eliminate a human teacher."
- **Ohio** — every district had a July 1, 2026 deadline to adopt a local AI-use policy (model policy covers student/staff use, privacy, vendor agreements).
- **Oklahoma** — SB 1734: AI in schools permitted only under educator supervision with human review, banned for high-stakes decisions, and requires **annual parent disclosure** of AI use — this is the closest any state has come to an age/parent-notice mechanism analogous to what this engine already does for privacy consent.
- Also active in 2026: Alabama, California, Mississippi, Oregon, Utah, Virginia, Washington (per MultiState/ExcelinEd trackers) — as of this pass, none amount to an outright student-facing generative-AI ban comparable to NYC's.
- None of the state-level K-12 AI policies found distinguish enforcement by student age bracket the way this engine's `under_13`/`under_16`/`under_18`/`adult` model does — Oklahoma's parent-disclosure duty is the closest analog (a compliance duty triggered by "student," not by age tier).
- Sources: [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2026/06/10/as-ai-use-in-schools-grows-lawmakers-and-districts-scramble-to-set-up-guardrails/), [ExcelinEd](https://excelined.org/2026/05/26/state-k-12-ai-policy-in-2026-milestones/), [MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/9/how-states-are-regulating-ai-in-education-this-legislative-session), [NASBE](https://www.nasbe.org/states-take-next-steps-on-governing-ai-use-in-schools/), [FutureEd tracker](https://www.future-ed.org/legislative-tracker-2026-state-ai-in-education-bills/), [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/AI_guidance_issued_by_state_departments_of_education).

**Applies to this product?** Indirectly and operationally — these are mostly sales/onboarding/procurement burdens (a district will ask for a policy-alignment statement, a vendor disclosure, an AI-coordinator point of contact) rather than something the compliance *engine* needs to encode per-jurisdiction. Worth tracking, not engine work.

### General-purpose (non-education-specific) state "AI companion chatbot" laws — the one that most plausibly touches `ai_chat` directly

Separately from K-12-specific rules, 12 states enacted **AI companion chatbot** consumer-protection laws in 2026. **California SB 243** (effective January 1, 2026) is the model: for any user an operator "knows" is a minor, it requires (a) clear, recurring disclosure that the user is talking to AI (at least every 3 hours generally, hourly reminders for minors in some state variants), (b) crisis-intervention protocols for self-harm signals, (c) filtering of sexually explicit content for minors. SB 243 exempts bots limited to customer service/internal business/technical assistance, and video-game bots restricted to game-related replies — **it does not contain an explicit educational-product exemption**, and at least one legal-industry source flags that "online learning platforms use chatbots that support students emotionally, encourage persistence, and maintain ongoing dialogue" as plausibly falling inside the "companion chatbot" definition (an AI system that "sustains a relationship" and "elicits an emotional response").

- Federal: the bipartisan **GUARD Act** (S.3062) — which would ban AI companion chatbots for minors outright federally and mandate age verification — passed the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously on April 30, 2026, and has a companion House bill (Moore/Foushee), but **has not been enacted**; full-Senate and House floor action are both pending.
- Sources: [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/), [MultiState — 12 states](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-105-state-ai-companion-chatbot-laws), [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law), [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html), [Global Policy Watch on GUARD Act](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-guard-act-regulating-minor-use-of-ai/), [Congress.gov S.3062](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text).

**Applies to this product?** This is the second most concrete finding. Unlike the education-specific bills above, this one is about the *technical/behavioral character of the chatbot itself* (does it sustain an ongoing relationship / elicit emotional response) rather than "is it used in a school," and it has **no clean carve-out for education**. Whether Peri's `ai_chat` mode is a "companion chatbot" under SB 243's definition is a real, open legal question that should get an actual answer rather than being assumed away by "it's for school."

## 2. UK Online Safety Act — Ofcom developments in 2026

- **Age-assurance enforcement is live and active.** The duty to run "highly effective age assurance" on the riskiest content took effect July 25, 2025; through the 2026 window covered here, Ofcom has opened investigations into 90+ platforms and issued fines (e.g., £800k against Kick Online Entertainment, £1m against an adult site). Ofcom committed to a statutory effectiveness report by end of July 2026, and a Parliament-facing rapid assessment of "what counts as highly effective age verification for under-16s" is due October 2026. The Category 1/2 categorisation register — which formally designates which services face the heaviest duties — has slipped to July 2026.
- **AI chatbots specifically:** Ofcom issued guidance on December 18, 2025 clarifying how the Online Safety Act applies to generative-AI chatbots. The key test is whether a chatbot is a **"user-to-user service"** — i.e., whether it lets users share AI-generated output *with other users*. A chatbot limited to one user talking to the bot itself, with no publishing/sharing to other users, sits outside that definition and is largely outside OSA's core content-moderation/age-assurance duties (it can still be captured other ways, e.g., if it generates pornographic content directly). Separately, in January 2026 Ofcom opened a formal investigation into **Novi Ltd**, an AI companion/character chatbot service (~6.5M global monthly users, 100k-300k in the UK), specifically over failure to implement effective age assurance against pornographic/harmful content generation.
- **Education exemption exists but is narrow and worth flagging, not resolving here.** Schedule 1 of the Online Safety Act 2023 exempts a user-to-user/search service "provided for education or childcare purposes by [a person] with legal responsibility for education or childcare" (or their employee). It is genuinely ambiguous whether this covers a third-party ed-tech *vendor's* product used inside a school (as opposed to a service the school itself operates) — this ambiguity has not been resolved in anything found in this pass and should not be assumed either way.
- Sources: [Ofcom age-checks](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-checks-to-protect-children-online), [Dentons — 2026-2027 milestones](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2026/may/21/ofcom-online-safety-enforcement-priorities-key-compliance-milestones-for-2026-2027), [Ofcom — Novi investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-ai-companion-chatbot-service), [SCL on Novi](https://www.scl.org/ofcom-investigates-ai-companion-chatbot-service/), [Inside Privacy — Ofcom AI chatbot guidance](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/ofcom-explains-how-the-uk-online-safety-act-will-apply-to-generative-ai/), [legislation.gov.uk Schedule 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/schedule/1).

**Applies to this product?** Low, on the facts as understood: Peri's `ai_chat` is architecturally a 1:1 student↔AI conversation with no sharing of AI output between students, which is the fact pattern Ofcom's December 2025 guidance treats as outside the "user-to-user service" definition that drives OSA's chatbot-specific duties — independent of whether the Schedule 1 education exemption would also apply. The Novi investigation is a materially different product (public-facing AI companion app, adult/self-harm content risk, no school supervision) and is not close on the facts. Recommend tracking, not acting — re-check if Ofcom broadens the "user-to-user" test or the October 2026 age-verification standard turns out to sweep in single-user AI chat.

## 3. Australia — Social Media Minimum Age law and the adjacent AI-chatbot angle

- **The under-16 social media ban is in force and being actively enforced**, not merely on paper. It took effect December 10, 2025 under the Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024. Platforms removed ~4.7 million under-16 accounts by mid-January 2026; eSafety judged this insufficient to meet the "reasonable steps" bar and, on March 31, 2026, opened formal investigations into Meta (Facebook/Instagram), Snapchat, TikTok, and YouTube. Penalties run up to A$49.5M. No penalties fall on the minors or parents themselves.
- **This law does not plausibly reach Peripateticware.** Its subject is age-restricted *social media platforms* — the operative definition and enforcement target is public social-networking-style services (accounts, feeds, follower/stranger contact). A closed, teacher-supervised K-12 tool with no public profile or feed is not the kind of service this Act or its enforcement actions are aimed at, and nothing found in this pass suggests eSafety is stretching the "social media" definition toward closed educational tools.
- **The genuinely adjacent development — separate legal mechanism, worth tracking:** Australia's **Age-Restricted Material Codes**, in force since March 9, 2026 (under the Online Safety Act, not the Minimum Age Act), extend age-verification obligations to, among other things, **AI chatbots "capable of generating explicit or harmful content."** eSafety has issued legal/transparency notices to AI companion providers (Character.AI, Chub AI, and others) demanding proof of age-assurance measures; Character.AI responded by adding age assurance and removing chat for its under-18 experience in Australia, while Chub AI geo-blocked Australia rather than comply. eSafety's own transparency report (Oct 2025 findings, expanded through 2026) found AI companion chatbots broadly failing to prevent sexual-content exposure and self-harm-adjacent interactions for minors.
- The Age-Restricted Material Codes test is **capability-based** ("capable of generating…"), not intent- or context-based — it does not ask whether the deployment is a supervised classroom tool. If Peripateticware's `ai_chat` is backed by a general-purpose LLM without hard content restrictions specific to explicit/harmful material, the literal test could theoretically apply regardless of the closed-classroom framing, though nothing found here suggests eSafety has targeted (or would plausibly target) a curriculum-bound K-12 assistant the way it has targeted public companion-chatbot apps.
- Sources: [Gadens overview](https://www.gadens.com/insights/no-likes-before-sixteen-australias-social-media-minimum-age-restriction), [infrastructure.gov.au](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications/internet/online-safety/social-media-minimum-age), [Xident — under-16 ban compliance](https://xident.io/blog/australia-social-media-minimum-age-ban-under-16-compliance-2026/), [eSafety — AI companion transparency notices](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-requires-providers-of-ai-companion-chatbots-to-explain-how-they-are-keeping-aussie-kids-safe), [eSafety — Oct 2025 findings](https://www.esafety.gov.au/industry/basic-online-safety-expectations/ai-services/findings-october-2025), [CADE — Age-Restricted Material Codes expansion to AI chatbots](https://cadeproject.org/updates/australia-expands-age-verification-rules-to-ai-chatbots-app-stores-and-online-platforms/), [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/03/02/no-id-no-ai-australia-tightens-the-net-on-chatbots/).

**Applies to this product?** The Minimum Age Act itself: **no** — squarely a social-media law, correctly excluded from further consideration despite superficial "AI + under-16" adjacency. The Age-Restricted Material Codes' chatbot provision: **track, don't dismiss** — it's a real, capability-based (not deployment-based) test that is closer to this product's actual feature than the headline social-media law is, and Australia's Privacy Act is already one of the 11 jurisdictions this engine models, so an Australian customer base is a live scenario, not hypothetical.

## 4. EU AI Act — the flag this repo already carries but never reads

Confirmed directly against the codebase for this task: `backend/config/jurisdictions/gdpr_eu.json` sets `"eu_ai_act_applies": true`, and `backend/services/privacy_engine.py` contains **no reference to `eu_ai_act_applies` anywhere** — it's inert configuration data with no code path, which matches what the task brief predicted.

What's changed in 2026, on the regulation itself:

- **August 2, 2026** is the key date: this is when the EU AI Act's high-risk-system obligations under Annex III come into full force (risk management, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, transparency to users, human oversight, accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity, conformity assessment, EU database registration). As of "now" (Sept 2026), this deadline has already passed.
- **Annex III, point 3 ("education and vocational training")** is scoped broadly — Commission guidance states it covers "both public and private institutions, with no limitation on the types or ages of pupils or students, or the specific environment (online, in person, in a blended mode, etc.)." The specific high-risk triggers are: (a) determining access/admission to an institution, (b) **evaluating learning outcomes, "including when used to steer the learning process"**, (c) assessing the appropriate education level for an individual, (d) monitoring/detecting prohibited student behavior during tests.
- **Already in force since February 2, 2025** (not new, but relevant and worth restating since it's easy to miss): Article 5(1)(f) flatly **bans emotion-recognition AI systems in educational settings**, with narrow safety/medical exceptions. If Peri does any sentiment/affect/emotion inference on student text or voice (as opposed to plain academic content understanding), that provision — not just Annex III — would be squarely in play, and it is a *prohibited practice*, not merely a high-risk compliance burden.
- Trade-press commentary on point (b) explicitly identifies personalized/adaptive tutoring as the ambiguous edge case: a system that dynamically adapts feedback based on student progress can read as high-risk if it's "steering the learning process," but would not if it neither grades nor determines access. This is not a settled question in general, let alone for this product specifically.
- Sources: [Modulos — Annex III draft guidelines](https://www.modulos.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-annex-iii-draft-guidelines-what-changed/), [artificialintelligenceact.eu Annex III text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/), [Pearl Cohen — next enforcement date](https://www.pearlcohen.com/new-guidance-under-the-eu-ai-act-ahead-of-its-next-enforcement-date/), [Plan Be Eco — EU AI Act for education 2026](https://planbe.eco/en/blog/eu-ai-act-for-the-education-industry/), [MmowW — high-risk education 2026](https://mmoww.net/ai/industry/eu-ai-act-high-risk-education/).

**Applies to this product?** This is the finding most worth a real (legal, not engineering) look soon, precisely *because* the codebase already has both the dangling `eu_ai_act_applies` flag and an EU/GDPR jurisdiction actively in production. Two separate questions need an actual answer, not an assumption:
1. Does Peri do anything resembling emotion/affect inference on students? If yes, that's a flat EU prohibition already in force, independent of the August 2026 Annex III deadline.
2. Does `ai_chat`'s behavior (adaptive follow-up questions, pacing, content selection) cross into "steering the learning process" under Annex III point 3(b)? This determines whether the platform needs Annex III's full high-risk compliance program (conformity assessment, technical file, human oversight design, etc.) for EU-market deployments.

Neither question can be answered by this research pass — they require legal interpretation against the actual behavior of the `ai_chat` implementation, which is out of scope here.

## Prioritized recommendations

**Worth actual near-term follow-up (legal/product review, not necessarily engineering yet):**
1. **Brazil's Digital ECA (Law 15.211/2025)** — already in force, ANPD actively enforcing, explicitly names chat features, age-banded. If there is or will be a Brazilian customer base, get a legal read on whether `ai_chat` triggers its age-verification/privacy-by-default duties.
2. **EU AI Act Annex III applicability** — the Aug 2, 2026 high-risk deadline has passed; the platform already has `eu_ai_act_applies: true` sitting unused in `gdpr_eu.json`. Get a legal determination of (a) whether Peri does anything that reads as emotion/affect inference (flatly banned since Feb 2025) and (b) whether `ai_chat`'s adaptive behavior is "steering the learning process" under Annex III point 3(b). The answer determines whether `eu_ai_act_applies` needs to start being read at all.
3. **California SB 243 "companion chatbot" classification** — no education carve-out exists in the statute; get a legal read on whether `ai_chat` meets the "sustains a relationship / elicits an emotional response" test, since if it does, disclosure-cadence and crisis-protocol duties apply specifically to minor users in California, which the engine's `under_18` bracket already tracks the seam for.
4. **NYC DOE generative-AI moratorium** — if NYC DOE is or could become a customer, this requires an explicit product/sales posture now: `curated_only` for 2K-8 and a real answer on whether `ai_chat` counts as a banned "companion chatbot" for high schoolers outside the five vetted-tool slots.

**Worth tracking, not urgent:**
- Federal GUARD Act (not yet enacted; would matter a great deal if it passes in its current form).
- Broader state K-12 AI procurement/disclosure legislation (Maryland, Illinois, Idaho, Ohio, Oklahoma, etc.) — mostly a sales/onboarding burden, not an engine-logic change.
- UK Ofcom's Oct 2026 age-verification standard-setting report and any revision of the "user-to-user service" chatbot test — currently doesn't reach a 1:1, non-sharing chat feature, but the test could move.
- Australia's Age-Restricted Material Codes AI-chatbot provision — capability-based, not deployment-based, so worth periodic re-check even though nothing suggests eSafety is currently looking at closed classroom tools.
- Singapore MOE guidance, South Africa's draft AI policy, Mexico's and Argentina's pending AI bills, Canada's post-AIDA vacuum — all pre-binding or non-education-specific; revisit each in 6 months.

**Sounds relevant, does not really apply to this product:**
- **Australia's Social Media Minimum Age law (the under-16 ban itself)** — squarely a public-social-media-platform law; a closed, teacher-supervised tool is not the kind of service its "reasonable steps"/account-holding framework or its enforcement actions (Meta, Snap, TikTok, YouTube) are aimed at. Don't let the under-16/AI-adjacency headline read imply otherwise — the *adjacent* Age-Restricted Material Codes item above is the one with real (if still speculative) teeth.
- **UK OSA's core chatbot/user-to-user regulation** — Peri's 1:1, non-sharing chat design is the fact pattern Ofcom's own December 2025 guidance treats as outside that definition, independent of the still-ambiguous Schedule 1 education exemption.
